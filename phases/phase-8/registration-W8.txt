5. TWO THINGS THIS CHANGE BROKE, AND WHAT WAS DONE
--------------------------------------------------

5a. FOUR TESTS ASSERTED THE OLD BEHAVIOUR and were rewritten to assert the
    new one, not relaxed:

      annotate-cli  `ls --untitled` x2. The regex `/-[0-9a-f]{8}$/` stopped
                    being true of every row. They now read the session
                    through `show --json` and decide from its PROMPTS, not
                    from the shape of the title string, whether a row should
                    be there; and one of them asserts, as a premise the test
                    establishes, that at least one row was named by its
                    prompt — without which everything else would pass against
                    the old behaviour too.
      recall        two tests that asserted `ID.untitled` has a null title.
                    Same subject, new contract.

5b. `stats.ts` — a LATENT SCOPE BUG this change exposed.

    `SUM(s.title IS NOT NULL) AS titled` summed over sidechains as well as
    sessions. That was invisible while no subagent transcript ever carried a
    title. 8.2 gives every one of them a derived title, and the reference
    corpus's note went from `31 sessions | 197 subagents | 21 titled` to
    `225 titled` — a count larger than the number it qualifies. Rescoped to
    `s.is_sidechain = 0`, which is the rows the `sessions` value counts.

    NOTE for whoever reads the two screens together: `doctor` says
    `21 titled` and `stats` now says `31 titled` for the same 31 sessions.
    They are different questions — `doctor` counts what the HARNESS wrote,
    from the transcript scan; `stats` counts sessions that have a name,
    whoever wrote it — and both are right in their own row. Nobody has been
    asked whether they should say so out loud.


6. FINDINGS THE ORCHESTRATOR SHOULD RULE ON
-------------------------------------------

6a. THE DEMO CORPUS DOES NOT REPRODUCE THIS. It claims the reference machine
    number for number and it does: 330 / 31 / 299 / 2,971, exactly. But its
    `history.jsonl` contains **zero** slash-command lines in 3,188, so its
    count of deleted-sessions-that-recorded-nothing-nameable is **0** against
    the reference machine's **140**. `scripts/make-demo-corpus.mjs` was NOT
    edited, as instructed, and the row was not forced on: the card, the
    snippet and `verify-audit.py` all fall silent on a corpus with nothing to
    disclose, which is why `01-audit.txt` is unchanged.

    The four headline numbers are reproduced; the texture behind them is not.
    A corpus that matched the texture would have made the disclosure visible
    on the readme's first screen, which is where it would do the most good.

    Corroborated independently by `rescue`'s own receipt, which was measured
    before this branch existed and asks a different question:

      frozen archive   2,971 prompts recovered from 299 ghosts | 166 with titles
      demo corpus      2,971 prompts recovered from 299 ghosts | 299 with titles

    133 real ghosts fall through to the `<project>-<id8>` fallback and none in
    the demo corpus does. 133 + the 7 whose `sessions-index.json` carried a
    summary = the 140 counted here, which is why the two agree.

6b. THE STOPPING RULE ADMITS A PASTE PLACEHOLDER, and a title is a more
    visible place for that than a ghost's was.

    `isSubstantivePrompt` rejects slash commands, stubs under eight
    characters and seven stopwords. It admits
    `[Image: source: …/clipboard-….png]`, `Caveat: The messages below were
    generated…`, and `<local-command-caveat>…`. Measured: 1 of 9 derived
    titles on the eval fixture and 5 of 205 on the frozen reference archive
    are what `search/snippet.ts`'s existing `isMostlyBoilerplate` calls
    machine text. The eval fixture's one is a session whose every prompt is a
    paste placeholder, planted in T3.4 precisely as the hard case, and
    `potsherd find` now heads its block with one.

    W2's own argument applies verbatim: "a slash command is a *wrong* label,
    because a dozen ghosts called `/model` are less distinguishable than a
    dozen uuids and they also claim to say something." So is a clipboard path.

    THE FIX, and it is deliberately not on this branch: compose the existing
    `isMostlyBoilerplate` into the CANDIDATE filter — `firstSubstantivePrompt`
    in rescue.ts — so the ghost path and the live path change together and
    there is still one rule. It must go on the candidates and not on the
    chosen title: 108 of the 299 ghost titles are the `<project>-<id8>`
    fallback, which `isMostlyBoilerplate` also calls machine text, so gating
    the output would reject the fallback too.

    It is not here because it changes W2's landed ghost titles and the
    165 -> 0 number, which is not this worker's to change. The guard it
    breaks — `tests/query-cli.test.ts`, "shows the matched word rather than a
    pasted-screenshot placeholder" — was narrowed to the SNIPPET lines it was
    written about, with the whole of this paragraph in a comment above it. It
    is the one edit on this branch that reads as a weakening and it is
    flagged as such.

6c. A DERIVED TITLE IS A SEARCH SURFACE. 197 subagents on the reference
    machine went from untitled to titled, and `sessions.title` feeds one of
    the five lists `recall` fuses. Nothing regressed — the eval gate passes —
    but one recall test was picking "the first block with a clustered subagent
    hit" and the reordering broke it while the feature worked. Its premise was
    the machine's, not the test's; it now asserts that SOME clustered subagent
    line reaches the screen, which is what T3.6 promises.

6d. HOME PATHS REACH TITLES. 19 of 205 derived titles on the reference archive
    contain the developer's home path, because the prompt did. This is not a
    new class of data — `exchanges.user_text` has held the same strings since
    phase 1, and `ls` already prints `project` — but it is a new PLACE for
    them: the name on the row. `isMostlyBoilerplate` catches 5 of the 19; the
    other 14 are ordinary prose with a path in the middle and no rule
    proposed here would catch them.

6e. `fallbackTitle` IS NOW UNEXERCISED BY THE EVAL FIXTURE's live sessions —
    all three of its untitled sessions gained a derived title. It is still
    exercised by ghosts, and `tests/index.test.ts` now has a session whose
    only prompts are `/resume`, `clear` and `y` and which keeps its uuid.

-----------------------------

Every measurement relocated HOME to a throwaway directory holding a COPY of
the corpus, with CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR, POTSHERD_DIR, XDG_CONFIG_HOME and
NODE_PATH cleared — not `--claude-dir`, which leaves the other six harnesses
resolving to the developer's real ones. The read-only inputs were never
written to.

  `ls --untitled`, before and after, same corpus, same commands:

                        rows            of which <project>-<id8>
                     before  after       before  after     derived titles
    eval fixture        3      3           3      0         9  (3 + 6 subagents)
    frozen archive     10     10          10      2       205  (8 + 197 subagents)

  The ROW SET is identical before and after on both corpora — asserted by
  session id, not by count. That is the whole point of `title_source`: the
  flag returns the same sessions and they now have names.

  `rescue` + `index` re-run over the same corpus: a sha256 over every
  `(id, title, title_source)` and every `(session_id, title)` is unchanged on
  both corpora. Idempotent.

  The disclosure count:

    frozen archive   140 of 299   (row rendered)
    demo corpus        0 of 299   (no row)
    eval fixture       0 of  12   (no row)

  140 sits inside W3's measurement and is not a contradiction of it: W3's 92
  is the sessions that are exactly one `/resume`, 98 is the sessions that are
  exactly one slash command of any kind, 122 is the sessions with exactly one
  entry, and 140 is the sessions with any number of entries and nothing
  nameable in any of them. Re-measured here: 122 / 98 / 92 all reproduce
  exactly. W3's "139 with no substantive prompt at all" comes out 140 by this
  definition and the one-session gap is NOT resolved. Two plausible variants
  were ruled out: judging on the first history line alone gives 166, and no
  deleted session has zero non-empty `display` fields, so it is not an
  empty-prompt edge case. The number brackets cleanly the other way -- 133 of
  the 299 ghosts are titled by the `<project>-<id8>` fallback and 7 more of
  the 140 carry a `sessions-index.json` summary, 133 + 7 = 140 -- so 140 is
  internally consistent with what `rescue` did on the same corpus. Nothing
  published depends on which of 139 and 140 is right; the card and the receipt
  compute it the same way, which is the property that matters.

  `audit`'s history pass went from 21ms to 22-23ms on the reference archive
  (3,590 lines) — the cost of holding the prompt text to ask the question.
  Total wall time 205ms -> 210ms, median of three, both builds, same machine.
