## C. the budget, before and after

Per reader the ceiling is `ASK_SESSION_CHARS = 8,000` and **it did not move**. The gap markers are
paid for *out of* the slice, not added to it (`windowOverhead(n) = 200 + (n+1)·56`), and
`windowCount` counts each window's own marker before deciding how many windows fit. The first
build of this did not, and came in at 8,268 characters against an 8,000 ceiling; the test that
catches it is `planWindows > stays under the ceiling at every window count, on every unit size`.

| | before | after `--windows 1` | after `--windows 5` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| readers | 5 | 6 | 6 |
| exchanges handed | 8 | 14 | **34** |
| total chars | 23,900 | 28,871 | **37,069** |
| ≈ tokens (chars/4) | 5,975 | 7,218 | **9,267** |
| largest reader | 8,013 | 8,013 | 8,013 |
| largest **windowed** reader | — | — | 7,862 |
| slice utilisation (of 6 × 8,000) | 50% | 60% | **77%** |

**Five windows cost more than one did, by +28.4% (28,871 → 37,069), and the shortlist fix costs a
further +20.8% (23,900 → 28,871). Run total +55.1%.** I am not going to dress that up: the
acceptance asks whether five windows cost more than one, and on this archive they do.

Why, precisely — and it is not that the cap moved. A single contiguous run *under-spends* its
allowance: before the change, three of five readers used 2.4–5.1 kB of an 8 kB slice, because a
session with one hit has one hit's worth of neighbours and then stops. Windows use the allowance
that was already authorised — utilisation goes 50% → 77% of a ceiling nobody changed. The trade is
**an unchanged per-reader ceiling at higher utilisation**, and what was bought with it is
8 exchanges → 34, from five places per long session instead of one, and a 123-exchange thread that
was previously not read at all.

At haiku-class reader pricing the whole difference is 3.3 k input tokens per `ask`. `--max-usd` is
untouched at $0.50 and no run came near it. If a caller wants the old number, `--windows 1` is
exactly it (28,871 with the better shortlist, 23,900 with neither).

**Latency.** The window path adds one session-scoped `recall` per long target. Measured in-process,
warm, three runs, no vectors on this index: `--windows 1` **142–151 ms**, `--windows 5`
**214–241 ms** — **+72 ms** for three extra recalls. Through the CLI, `--readers-out` wall time is
**0.39–0.41 s** (the audit measured 0.36 s before the change). Model calls: **0**, both ways.

## D. the default, and the short-thread boundary

**`ASK_WINDOWS = 5`.** The number is the audit's — §4 item 3 asks for `--windows 5` and §2 F5
argues it. What I can add is that five is also what the existing budget pays for without lowering
any floor:

```
(ASK_SESSION_CHARS − windowOverhead(5)) / 5 = (8,000 − 536) / 5 = 1,493 chars per window
1,493 / (MIN_UNIT_CHARS + UNIT_HEADER_CHARS) = 1,493 / 724 = 2.06 exchanges per window
```

So at five, every window still affords its seed **and** one neighbour at the existing
700-character floor — which is the `±1` rule `excerptUnits` has always had, and the reason it has
it (*a decision is stated in the exchange after the one that raised it*). At **eight** the share
is 933 and a window is one exchange with no context; the neighbour rule dies silently. At **three**
the shares are fat and the coverage is thin, which is the failure the audit measured, three times
instead of once. Measured on the archive, on `LONG`: at `--windows 3` the shares are fat enough for
full seed ±1 triples (`5,6,7 · 11,12,13 · 118,119`) but only three places are looked at; at **`--windows 5`** every window is a seed and one
neighbour (`1,2 · 5,6 · 11,12 · 55,56 · 118,119`); at **`--windows 8`** five of the eight windows
are **bare singletons** (`1 · 6 · 12 · 20,21 · 56 · 94 · 100,101 · 118,119`) — the neighbour rule
has died, exactly where the arithmetic says it should. Five is the corner.
`tests/windows.test.ts > the constants > keeps five windows affordable at the existing floor`
fails if it moves.

**The short-thread boundary is `WINDOW_MIN_EXCHANGES = 12`, and it is derived, not chosen.** One
slice is 8,000 characters; no exchange may be cut below `MIN_UNIT_CHARS = 700` plus its
24-character `[seq n · date]` header. So a slice holds at most `8,000 / 724 = 11.0` exchanges — **a
session of eleven exchanges or fewer fits in the budget whole.** Below that, windowing cannot add
information and can only add markers for text the reader could have had. Twelve is the first size
at which something *must* be left out, and therefore the first at which *which* part is left out
is a decision worth making.

Below the boundary `ask` does not merely produce one window — it takes **the pre-F5 code path**,
`excerptUnits`, unchanged. The reader's block carries no marker and no preamble. That is acceptance
6 discharged by construction rather than by care. Evidence: the committed synthetic demo corpus
(31 sessions, longest 12 exchanges) produces six readers, `1 window` each, zero markers, identical
seqs to before. It is also why F5 could never have been found on the demo corpus — nothing in it is
long enough to window.

`--windows n` caps at three ceilings and takes the smallest: the ask, the material
(one window per 12 exchanges), and the budget (`--windows 100` yields 9, not 100 headers with
nothing under them).

## E. should `ask`'s shortlist run at the confidence floor? — a view, no change made

**No. Leave it off, and windowing is now a second, independent reason.**

The reason T10.2 gave still stands: `ask` hands rows to a reader that is asked to judge, and an
uncalibrated floor upstream of a calibrated reader discards exactly what the reader exists to
reject. Windowing does not weaken that; it adds a new argument on the same side, plus one honest
caveat.

*The new argument.* A window seed is **not a claim**. Before F5, a weak hit was the entire excerpt
a session got, so a weak hit cost the reader a whole reader. Now a weak hit costs **one window of
five** — 20% of one reader's slice — and the other four are unaffected. Meanwhile, withholding it
does not save that window: it hands it to the `spread` backfill, which is a *coverage* guess and
strictly less informed than a weak lexical match. The floor would trade a weak signal for no
signal at the same price. That is a worse deal after F5 than before it.

*The caveat, so this is not one-sided.* Thread expansion admits relatives at score 0 — they are
read because they hold the same work, not because they matched — so the shortlist now contains
rows that no floor would have passed anyway. If a floor were ever turned on for `ask`, it would
have to be applied to the *hits*, before expansion, or it would delete the fix in §B. Worth
recording before somebody wires F1's floor through this function.

*What I did not do.* I did not change it, and I cannot settle it here: with no model I can measure
what a reader was handed, never what it answered. The measurement that would settle it is
`evals/ask-run.ts` at `minConfidence` on and off with the same windows — T10.10's ground.
