## 0. The one-paragraph version

RRF is a function of rank alone, so `find`'s score cannot say whether the archive answers
the question. This adds a second axis computed from the evidence the fusion discards, and a
floor to return nothing under. It works, and it works decisively, **for short distinctive
queries** — the class the audit's three probes are in. It does **not** work for long
natural-language queries, and I could not make it work without fitting a constant to the
exam. §d has the numbers and the lever.

### What it is computed from (requirement 4)

```
calibrated = coverage × (0.60 + 0.25 × strength + 0.15 × agreement)
```

- **coverage** — of the query's distinctive words (`quotableTokens`, the same list the snippet
  highlighter uses), what fraction this block can actually show. Counted over the union of its
  hits' text **and its title**, with `wordMatchesToken`, so the label and the highlighted words
  on screen are one measurement.
- **strength** — `from[].raw`: the list's own bm25/cosine for this row, over the best magnitude
  that *same list* produced for this *same query*. Relative, never absolute.
- **agreement** — distinct lists that found it, over 3 (the most a `--no-embed` index can give).

The three weights are a partition of 1, so **coverage is a ceiling**: no corroboration and no
bm25 magnitude can lift a row whose words are not there. That is the cliff, and it is the one
property the audit's prescribed fix (normalise the fused score) cannot have.

The floors were **recorded, not fitted**. `WEAK_FLOOR = 0.5` because at a rank-1 row of one
uncorroborated list (multiplier 0.85) it says *a row must show a clear majority of your
distinctive words* — 2 of 3, 3 of 4, 3 of 5; 2 of 4 is out. `STRONG_FLOOR = 0.75` because at
the same multiplier it says *essentially all of them, in one conversation*. Neither was moved
after any measurement. This is the same stopping-rule discipline phase 3 applied to
`WEIGHTS.vec_* = 1.5`, and it is stated in the source.

## d. What I could not do, and why — read this one

### d1. Lexical calibration cannot separate long natural-language queries. Measured.

The floor is on by default in `find`. On the committed eval fixture, at that floor:

| | |
|---|---|
| eval queries returning an **empty page** | **17 of 25** |
| rank-1 answers **lost** | 3 of 7 |
| rank-≤5 answers lost | 7 of 14 |

Separability, over the 25 eval queries, bm25-only, `calibrated` of the **correct** session
against the best **wrong** session:

| | n | min | median | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| correct answer | 19 | 0.000 | **0.370** | 0.771 |
| best wrong answer | 25 | 0.000 | **0.264** | 0.567 |

**The distributions overlap almost completely. There is no threshold that keeps the correct
answers and drops the wrong ones for this query class.** Per the brief's ruling I am
reporting that rather than tuning until it comes out right.

Two things make this less alarming than it looks, and one makes it worse:

1. **The eval set is adversarial to this measure by construction.** `evals/run.ts` flags any
   query sharing more than **0.6** of its content words with its own answer, and the set was
   rewritten to sit under that line. My floor admits a row at coverage ≳ 0.59. The eval set
   deliberately excludes exactly the queries the floor admits. The two numbers are the same
   number, approached from opposite sides.
2. **The vector half does not rescue it.** I checked, because it was the obvious fix. With
   embeddings on, cosine for the correct session runs 0.431–0.770 and for the best wrong
   session 0.526–0.744 — also total overlap. A cosine floor would additionally be an absolute
   magnitude, which the brief told me to avoid. **So I did not add a semantic lane**, and the
   measurement is the reason.
3. **It is worse than the table suggests for a human.** `potsherd find "the pod kept getting
   killed even though the app was fine"` now prints `no match` where it used to print 8 rows.
   The rows were wrong (F8 measured that separately) and the new last line teaches the winning
   strategy — but it is two commands where there was one.

I also tested and rejected **IDF-weighted coverage**, which was the principled candidate.
Measured document frequencies on the fixture: `the` 152, `pod` 4, `kept` 0, `killed` 1,
`fine` 0, `kubernetes` 1, `ingress` 0. On a corpus this size IDF is noise — `kept`, `even`
and `fine` have df 0 and would get the *highest* weight, making the natural-language case
strictly worse. Cost was not the blocker (3 ms for 20 terms); discrimination was. On a 433 MB
archive every English word has df > 0 and the denominator returns to its full length, so the
rule would also be scale-dependent, which the brief forbids.

**The lever, if you want it off.** One line, `packages/cli/src/commands/find.ts`, in
`minConfidence()`: change `default: return 'weak';` to `default: return 'none';`. Everything
stays labelled, in both views; nothing is withheld. The three eval controls would then need
`--min-confidence weak` to keep meaning what they mean. My recommendation is to **ship it
on** — it is what the audit asked for and what the ACCEPT criteria require — and to treat
T10.9 (keyphrase extraction from long queries) as the thing that closes the gap, since it
turns a sentence into the 2–4 distinctive nouns the floor is good at.

### d5. Threshold portability to a large corpus.

Every input is a ratio against something the same query produced on the same index — coverage
is a fraction of the query, strength is a fraction of its own list's best, agreement is a
count of lists. No absolute magnitude appears anywhere, by design. So I expect the floors to
hold at 433 MB. **I could not verify that**, and one thing genuinely could move: block
coverage is measured over a block's ≤3 kept hits plus its title, and on a corpus with
150-exchange sessions a true topic may spread its words across exchanges that did not become
hits — which would *depress* coverage and withhold true rows. Worth re-measuring on the real
archive. The cheap mitigation, if it bites, is to raise `perSession` for the coverage pass
only.

### d6. Defects found outside my scope, not fixed

- **`recall()`'s floor is off for `ask` and `graft` by design** (they hand rows to a reader
  who can judge). That is a defensible default but it means `ask`'s shortlist is still
  uncalibrated. T10.5 owns the windows; somebody should decide whether `ask` wants the floor.
- **`--explain`'s ledger says nothing about confidence.** It explains the ranker in full and
  is now explaining only half the screen. Small, and squarely T10.x's.
- **The empty screen prints two different "text search only" sentences** depending on whether
  the result was empty, one of which recommends `--vectors on` when there is no model. Pre-
  existing, cosmetic, not mine.
