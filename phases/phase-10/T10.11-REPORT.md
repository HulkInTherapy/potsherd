## 2. The refit, and its method

### What changed

| | was | is |
|---|---|---|
| `PIPELINE_COST_FACTOR` (`llm.ts`, new) | — (implicitly 1.0) | **1.5**, on **money only**, on **derived** call counts only |
| `ESTIMATOR_FIT` (`llm.ts`, new) | — | `'2026-08-23T12:00:00.000Z'` |
| `readCalibration` (`calibration.ts`) | any complete row | complete rows **at or after `ESTIMATOR_FIT`** |
| `CALL_PROFILES` latency (`baseMs`, `msPerKChar`, `parallelEfficiency`) | 46 200 / 915 / 0.8 | **unchanged** |
| `CALL_PROFILES` price (`baseUsd`, `usdPerMChar`) | 0.016 / 1.057 | **unchanged** |
| `spread` | 0.8–2.0 time, 0.8–1.4 money | **unchanged** |

### Why the correction is not on the per-call price

The per-call price has an independent check the run-level ratios do not. Given the **true** call
count and true mean prompt size, the fit reproduces the 21 aug 209-call run at **$11.18 against a
real $12.93 — 1.16× under**, not 1.7×. The twelve-call price fit is close. Moving it would throw
away a direct measurement in order to absorb a residual that belongs somewhere else, and would
turn that run's quote into a **1.30× over**.

Where the residual belongs is the **call arithmetic**. `estimate()` derives calls as
`chunks === 1 ? 1 : chunks + 1` — the *ideal* pipeline. The real one, in `cards/pipeline.ts` and
`cards/extract.ts`, also pays for a **supplement pass** (at most one extra call per card,
re-sending the units coverage says the first pass missed) and for **retries**
(`into.calls += r.attempts ?? 1`), and prices neither. Both can only *add* calls and characters.
That is the shape of a one-directional under-quote, and it is the shape `08` row 6 recorded.

### The stopping rule, and why that number

`pipeline.ts` allows **at most one** supplement per card. So the work the ideal arithmetic omits
is bounded **below by nothing** (no card supplemented) and **above by a second full call on every
card** — the interval **[1×, 2×]**, fixed by the pipeline's own code and not by any sample.

> **1.5 is the midpoint of that interval.**

That is the whole derivation. It is **not** the value that minimises residual on the two runs
that check it (that value is **1.66**), and it was chosen from the code before the rows were
consulted; the rows were then used only to *check* it. This is the same direction of inference
that made `1.5` a stopping rule elsewhere in this project rather than an argmax — and with two
samples an argmax would be indistinguishable from fitting the test set.

`tests/llm.test.ts` asserts `PIPELINE_COST_FACTOR === 1.5`, asserts it lies in `(1, 2]`, and
asserts it is **strictly below** the argmax computed from the recorded runs in the same test.

### Why the clock is untouched

The 23 aug run — the only one whose scope was not truncated away — came in at **1.15× of its
quoted wall time** against **1.63× of its quoted money**. Those are not the same error seen
twice. Extra calls join the concurrency gate and overlap with the fan-out already in flight, so
they cost wall time at the marginal rate and money at the full rate. Correcting both axes by the
money factor would have quoted that run's 10m 8s as 13m 15s — the "now 2× pessimistic" outcome
that acceptance 3 rules out. **The latency fit stays exactly as it was.**

### The fit epoch, and a defect it closes

A `card_runs` row records what *that* run was told by the constants compiled that day. Re-fit the
constants and the row becomes a measurement of a **different estimator**; averaging it into a
correction on top of the new one applies the same error twice. This is not hypothetical — the two
rows on this machine are the evidence for `PIPELINE_COST_FACTOR`, and left unfiltered they would
multiply the already-corrected quote by 1.66 again.

`ESTIMATOR_FIT` is therefore the identity of the compiled fit, `readCalibration` ignores older
rows, and the constant moves in the same commit as any constant above it. Its value sits
immediately **after** the last row it was derived from (23 aug 11:30 UTC) rather than at the
wall-clock moment of the edit: a boundary in the future would also exclude the next run a user
makes.

**Consequence, stated plainly:** after this change `readCalibration()` still returns `null` on the
reference machine. Its two runs are evidence *for* the constants, not a correction *on* them.

## 3. The direction, before and after

Signed error = (actual ÷ quote) − 1, per call. Positive = **the quote was too low**.

| run | quote before | quote after | actual | **signed error before** | **signed error after** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 22 aug | $0.93 | $1.39 | $0.96 | **+70 %** | **+13 %** |
| 23 aug | $1.76 | $2.63 | $2.94 | **+63 %** | **+9 %** |

**It still errs low, by about a tenth, and that is deliberate.** Two reasons:

1. **It must not err high.** A factor of 2 — or the 1.66 argmax rounded up — puts both residuals
   *below* 1, and an estimator that over-quotes is a different failure, not a fixed one. 1.5 is
   the largest value in the structural interval that leaves both runs still (just) under-quoted,
   which is the only side of 1.0 that a range can cover honestly.
2. **What a ceiling must be safe against is the top of the range, not the point.** And this is the
   part that was actually broken:

   | run | `usdHigh` (point × 1.4) **before** | actual | covered? | **after** | covered? |
   |---|---:|---:|:--:|---:|:--:|
   | 22 aug | $1.30 | $0.96 (11 of 18 calls) | per-call ratio 1.70 > 1.4 → **no** | $1.95 | **yes** (1.13) |
   | 23 aug | $2.46 | $2.94 | **no** | $3.69 | **yes** (1.09) |

   Before the refit the **honest range itself under-quoted both runs**. No reading of that card
   would have warned anybody, which is exactly `08` item 20: *an estimate that is too low is not
   catchable by any pre-call gate.* After it, both land inside `[usdLow, usdHigh]` with margin,
   for the first time. `tests/llm.test.ts` asserts both halves of that sentence.

Time, for completeness and unchanged by this task: −9 % on the truncated run's total (the quote
was *over*), +15 % on the un-truncated one.

## 5. The zero-cost rungs

### What `estimate()` returns

| rung | how it is asked for | `usd` | `chargeable` | `seconds` | `basis` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — host-agent seam | `hostSeam: true` (**new**) | **0** (low and high 0 too) | **false** | **0** | `HOST_SEAM_BASIS` |
| 2 — `claude -p` | `backend: 'claude-cli'` | api-equivalent, > 0 | false | > 0 | `est. — the agent-sdk fit for the same binary, spawned directly` |

**The rung-1 defect this closes.** `RESOLUTION_LADDER`'s rung 1 has `backend: null` by
construction — there is no transport to name. So a caller quoting that path passes no `backend`,
and `estimate()`'s old default was
`chargeable = input.backend ? input.backend === 'api' : true` → **`true`**, on the agent-sdk
profile. The one path on which potsherd makes **no model call at all** was the one path that
quoted the user a chargeable bill and an agent-sdk wall time it does not spend. It now returns
zero dollars, zero potsherd seconds, `chargeable: false`, and keeps `calls`, `inputTokens` and
`outputTokens` — which is the unit that is actually left.

### Is `$0.00` the right thing to print? No.

`$0.00` is arithmetically true and still misleading: it reads as *"this is free"*, when what is
true is *"potsherd is not the one spending"*. The prompts still have to be answered, out of the
host agent's context, on the user's own subscription — and on a large `card --all` that context
is the binding constraint, not money. A dollar row of `$0.00` puts the reader's attention on the
one quantity that cannot bind.

**Recommendation (not implemented — `render/estimate.ts` and `packages/cli/` are outside this
task's file list).** On rung 1 only, replace the money row rather than zeroing it. Exact text:

- label: `model calls` → value: `none by potsherd`
- note: `the host agent answers · <compact(inputTokens)> tokens of its context`

and drop the `estimated time` row on that rung, since the seconds are the host's turn and not
something potsherd can predict. The `basis` line already carries the sentence for it:

```
the host agent answers — potsherd makes no model call, so the unit is tokens, not dollars
```

**Rung 2 (`claude -p`) needs no change and should keep its dollar figure.** potsherd really does
make those calls, so the seconds are real and the api-equivalent is a truthful "what this would
have cost on a key" — the number that lets somebody decide whether to buy one. It already prints
`$0 charged` beside it and already carries the wider `est.` range for a profile that has never
been measured at card size.

## 6. What I could not do

- **Fit anything.** Two runs, both aborted, one machine, one backend, one model, 22 hours apart.
  What is here is a **direction fixed by a structurally-bounded constant and checked against the
  runs**, not a fit. The ruling in the task said reporting that is a complete answer, and this
  section is me taking it.
- **Generate fresh data.** No API key, no SDK. The `claude -p` rung works here, but a handful of
  runs is a handful and would not have changed any of the above; the honest missing input is 5
  *complete* runs, which needs a corpus small enough to finish under a ceiling.
- **Separate "more calls than predicted" from "more chars per call than predicted".** Run 2 made
  36 calls against 35 quoted, but finished only 3 of 8 targets — the equality is where the abort
  happened to land, not agreement. Two truncated runs cannot identify which term is wrong, which
  is *why* the correction is a single money factor rather than a change to one coefficient
  pretending to know.

## 7. Defects found, not fixed (outside scope)

1. **Nothing passes `hostSeam` yet.** `cards/plan.ts` and `packages/cli/src/commands/card.ts` are
   reserved, so the rung-1 quote path exists in `estimate()` and has no caller. Whoever wires the
   seam's `card`/`ask` quote should pass `hostSeam: true` when `topRung()` is `host-seam`.
2. **`card_runs` stores the spend at the abort but not the projection that caused it.**
   `BudgetError.detail.projected` is the number the gate compared and the number a user sees; it
   is lost. That is why the plan's `$3.01` cannot be checked against the store. One more column
   on `card_runs` would make ceiling stops auditable (`db.ts` is reserved).
3. **Rule 1 throws away the run that matters most.** A run stopped by `--max-usd` is the exact
   event `08` item 20 is about, and `complete = 0` makes it evidence about nothing — even though
   its *per-call* money ratio is perfectly interpretable (it is how every number in §1 was
   computed). Relaxing rule 1 to admit incomplete rows **on the money axis, per call** would let
   a machine learn from its own ceiling stops. Not done here: combined with `ESTIMATOR_FIT` it
   would have had no effect on this machine today, and it is a semantic change to a rule another
   worker's half of the file also leans on.
